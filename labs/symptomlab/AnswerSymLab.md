Advanced Driveability Symptom Taxonomy, Exhaust Gas Chemistry Ground Truths, and Context-Modifier Dynamics for Petrol Engine Diagnostics (1990–2010)Diagnostic architectures for passenger petrol vehicles manufactured between 1990 and 2010 (Euro 1 to Euro 5 emission standards) require translating subjective, driver-reported symptoms into precise physical fault families. Legacy diagnostic channels frequently struggle with structural flaws, specifically the conflation of primary symptoms with environmental context variables. By restructuring the symptom catalog into a non-overlapping two-tier taxonomy, these dimensional overlaps are eliminated, preserving the statistical power of diagnostic prior probabilities.Under warm engine operating conditions, post-catalyst exhaust-gas chemistry—consisting of unburned hydrocarbons ($HC$), carbon monoxide ($CO$), nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$), and the air-fuel equivalence ratio ($\lambda$)—acts as the absolute diagnostic ground truth. The three-way catalytic converter (TWC) requires $\lambda$ to remain within a narrow operating window between $0.995$ and $1.005$ to effectively oxidize $HC$ and $CO$ while reducing $NO_x$. When component degradation shifts the air-fuel mixture away from stoichiometry, the engine emits highly specific tailpipe chemical footprints.Exhaust Gas Chemistry Footprints for Refined Component Fault FamiliesIn a fully functioning baseline state, a certified passenger petrol engine of this era yields post-catalyst emissions of approximately $0.15 \text{ g/km}$ of $HC$, $0.61 \text{ g/km}$ of $CO$, and $0.26 \text{ g/km}$ of $NO_x$, with a baseline fuel consumption of $11.01 \text{ liters per 100 km}$. When an ignition coil experiences secondary-insulation breakdown under load, the high-voltage current arcs to the engine block rather than jumping the spark plug gap. This prevents spark ignition, letting a completely unburned fuel-air charge enter the exhaust manifold.While a raw, pre-catalyst misfire spikes unburned hydrocarbons above $1000 \text{ ppm}$, the post-catalyst tailpipe signature shows $0\%$ $HC$ change under light misfire loads, provided the converter remains above its light-off temperature of $200^\circ\text{C}$ to $225^\circ\text{C}$. This post-combustion oxidation process consumes available oxygen, suppressing the converter's ability to reduce $NO_x$, which leads to a $40\%$ reduction in post-catalyst $NO_x$ and a $60\%$ increase in $CO$ due to partial oxidation.Vacuum leaks introduce unmetered air downstream of the throttle plate, bypassing the MAF sensor. Because the ECU commands fuel based on under-reported air mass, the cylinder operates under lean conditions ($\lambda > 1.05$). Lean mixtures burn slowly, causing a "lean misfire" if the limits of flammability are exceeded. This raises unburned $HC$ emissions.In slightly lean zones, excess oxygen reacts with nitrogen at high combustion temperatures, raising engine-out $NO_x$ to over $1,500 \text{ ppm}$. If the catalytic converter experiences thermal melting or chemical poisoning, its active metal sites (platinum, palladium, rhodium) are compromised. Post-catalyst emissions quickly approach engine-out levels.Cruising operations exhibit a $297\%$ increase in $HC$, a $211\%$ increase in $CO$, and a $378\%$ increase in $NO_x$. Transient driving cycle simulations show overall increases of $251\%$ $HC$, $225\%$ $CO$, and $336\%$ $NO_x$, reflecting a near-complete loss of conversion efficiency.Mass-airflow sensor contamination acts as an insulator on the hot-wire resistor, reducing heat dissipation to the incoming air charge. The ECU under-reports the air mass, shortening the injector pulse width and causing a lean shift. Under steady-state operations, the resulting mixture adjustments cause a $+168\%$ increase in unburned $HC$, a $+782\%$ spike in $CO$ as the system swings to rich corrections, and a $-86\%$ drop in $NO_x$.EVAP purge valve failures disrupt closed-loop fuel control by introducing fuel vapors directly into the intake manifold. When stuck open, these vapors bypass throttle metering, flooding the engine with fuel during idle.The short-term fuel trim drops below $-10\%$ as the ECU attempts to restore stoichiometry. This rich shift causes a $+200 \text{ ppm}$ increase in tailpipe $HC$, while overall fuel economy drops by $5\%$ to $15\%$ due to unmetered vapor consumption.Piston ring or valve-stem seal wear allows heavy lubricating oil to enter the combustion chamber. These high-molecular-weight hydrocarbons do not burn completely, increasing tailpipe $HC$ by $+300 \text{ ppm}$ and raising $CO$ to $+1.5\%$. The exhaust displays a blue-grey smoke with a strong oil-burning odor.Head gasket failures introduce coolant into the cylinders, causing thermal quenching. This lowers peak combustion temperatures, reducing $NO_x$ by $50\%$, while flame-front quenching increases tailpipe $HC$.A stuck-open or leaking fuel injector drips raw fuel directly into the cylinder instead of atomizing it into a fine mist. This creates fuel-rich zones, dropping fuel economy by $15\%$ to $30\%$ and raising tailpipe $NO_x$ to $+352\%$ under high exhaust temperatures.Finally, a coolant temperature sensor biased "cold" tricks the ECU into running in open-loop cold-enrichment mode. The ECU injects massive amounts of excess fuel, dropping $\lambda$ to $0.77$ and raising tailpipe $CO$ to $8.49\%$. This indicates that unmetered air entering through degraded gaskets is a much more common cause of idle instability than primary ignition failures.Environmental Context-Modifier Dynamics and Physical RationalesEngine temperature and load states directly affect petrol engine systems, allowing the diagnostic engine to dynamically adjust the prior probabilities of specific faults.Thermal Expansion and Contraction Effects on SealsElastomeric and paper gaskets (such as intake manifold and throttle body seals) shrink in cold weather and expand as the engine warms up. This physical behavior directly affects vacuum leak diagnostic priors:$$\text{Leak Ingress Area} \propto \frac{1}{\text{Engine Temperature}}$$When an owner reports a rough idle under occurs_during = cold_only, the diagnostic engine promotes the probability of a structural vacuum leak. As the engine reaches its operating temperature, the manifold gaskets expand, sealing the leak and resolving the symptom.In contrast, electronic components like crankshaft position sensors and ignition coils suffer from thermal insulation breakdown. As temperature rises, internal electrical resistance in the copper windings increases, leading to open circuits or weak sparks. Under occurs_during = hot_only, the system demotes vacuum leaks and promotes ignition coil and position sensor failures.Pressure and Electrical Stress Relationships Across Load StatesFor petrol engines, manifold absolute pressure ($MAP$) behaves inversely to throttle valve opening. Manifold vacuum is expressed as:$$\text{Vacuum} \propto \frac{1}{\text{Throttle Valve Opening}}$$This physical relationship governs the behavior of the load_state dimension:Load State = Idle or Coasting Deceleration (High Manifold Vacuum): With the throttle valve closed, manifold vacuum is at its peak ($18 \text{ to } 22 \text{ in.-Hg}$). The high pressure differential between the atmosphere and the intake tract pulls unmetered air through any compromised gaskets, making the engine highly sensitive to vacuum leaks. Under these states, the diagnostic engine promotes vacuum leaks and carbon-clogged IACV faults.Load State = Transient Acceleration (Low Manifold Vacuum / High Cylinder Pressure): When the throttle valve opens, manifold vacuum drops close to zero, and absolute cylinder pressure rises sharply. High cylinder pressure increases the density of the air-fuel mixture, raising the dielectric resistance across the spark plug gap. The ignition coil must supply a much higher voltage (up to $40,000 \text{ V}$) to fire the plug. This extreme dielectric stress causes the high-voltage current to puncture degraded coil insulation and arc directly to the engine block, resulting in a sudden, sharp jerk or power stumble. Under load_state = transient_acceleration, the system promotes ignition coil insulation breakdown and demotes minor vacuum leaks, as the open throttle plate swallows any minor unmetered air ingress.Pulse-Width Modulation and Vapor Purge DynamicsThe canister purge valve controls the flow of stored fuel vapors into the intake manifold using Pulse-Width Modulation (PWM). The ECU varies the valve's duty cycle between $5\%$ and $50\%$ depending on engine speed and load:                ---> Duty Cycle: 10% to 15% ---> Low Vapor Volume Introduced
     ---> Duty Cycle: 30% to 40% ---> Steady Combustion of Fuel Vapors
    ---> Duty Cycle: 0% (Closed) ---> Purge Suspended for Precise Fueling
At idle, a duty cycle of $10\%$ to $15\%$ is applied, letting a small volume of vapor enter the combustion chamber. Under steady highway cruise (load_state = steady_cruise), the duty cycle rises to $30\%$ to $40\%$ as the engine easily accommodates the additional fuel vapors.Under transient acceleration (load_state = transient_acceleration), the ECU completely closes the purge valve to maintain precise fuel control. RecommendationsTo maximize diagnostic accuracy within the specified operating envelope (passenger petrol vehicles, model years 1990–2010, Euro 1 to Euro 5, warm engine), the following system architecture updates are recommended:Incorporate the load_state Context Axis: Modify the database schema and user interface to include a third environmental axis: load_state with the values idle, steady_cruise, transient_acceleration, and coasting_deceleration. This enables the diagnostic system to separate static air-metering faults from transient ignition failures.Establish a 35% Trust Ceiling on Heuristic Priors: Service records show that "Cannot Duplicate" (CND) or "No Fault Found" (NFF) rates range between $15\%$ and $40\%$ for subjective driveability symptoms. The diagnostic engine should limit the maximum mathematical weight assigned to any heuristic prior to $65\%$. If a customer's raw report is weighted too heavily, the system will misdiagnose vehicles with vague or intermittent complaints.Adopt the Revised Symptom Taxonomy: Replace the legacy 18-pair catalog with the revised two-tier symptom taxonomy. This structure removes redundant temperature filters, eliminating mathematical collinearity in the prior, and introduces highly predictive SAE-defined symptoms (including surging idle, prolonged crank, and tip-in hesitation).Implement a Commercial Data Pipeline for Empirical Validation: Because open-source academic and public datasets lack raw driver-reported symptoms paired with verified repairs, a commercial API license should be secured for the CarMD Vehicle Health API or the iATN TechHelp FIX Database. This paired, real-world data is required to dynamically train and optimize the diagnostic prior probabilities over time.
